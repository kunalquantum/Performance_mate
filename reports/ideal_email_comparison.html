What each email is best at

Email A is the tight-list, single-persona template

  • Best when: targeting one institution, or a persona-tight slice within one, where you can name the dominant persona confidently.
  • Wins: 56.5% open · 100% CTOR · 0 bounces · more absolute clicks than B despite a 6.8× smaller list.
  • Uses: a hard number as anchor, product named mid-body, low-friction "connect with us" CTA.
  • Costs: per-institution effort — you have to know the persona mix before you send.

Email B is the broadcast, multi-persona template

  • Best when: you have volume and a mixed list — cross-institution blast where you cannot pre-know the persona.
  • Wins: 22% open on 149 delivered is still above the 20% SaaS benchmark — genuinely respectable at scale. Reaches Research + Finance + IT in one message.
  • Uses: bulleted pain list scans well, multi-persona vocabulary widens the trigger surface.
  • Costs: 4.5% bounce rate is above the 3% deliverability threshold, no hard number in body, product unnamed, CTA points to homepage not a conversation — hence 27% CTOR vs 100%.

Verdict — which is the "ideal email"?

Per-send effectiveness: Email A wins on every ratio (open · click · CTOR · deliverability). If the goal is the highest-converting pattern, copy Email A.

Reach at volume: Email B proves the pattern scales — 22% open on 149 delivered is genuinely good in the broadcast world. If the goal is Monday-morning volume, Email B is the working template — with three fixes.

Recommendation: keep both templates. Use A for named-institution outreach. Use B for cross-institution blasts after the three fixes below.

Three fixes to bring Email B closer to Email A

  • 1 · Fix the merge tag. Change $[UD:FIRST_NAME||]$ to a merge that has a fallback ("Hi there," when the field is blank). Raw tokens on failed merges kill open credibility and are almost certainly part of the 4.5% bounce impression.
  • 2 · Add one hard number to the body. Email A anchors on "over 25%". Email B has none — the reader has nothing to weigh. Try "over 25% more proposals submitted" or "recover XX% of admin time". Repeat it as a standalone highlighted line, like A does.
  • 3 · Replace the homepage URL with a conversation CTA. "see: www.grantsnow.co.uk" points the reader to a homepage they have to explore. Change to "We'd be happy to share a short overview — Connect with us." That single change is the difference between A's 100% CTOR and B's 27%.

Sub-3% bounce rate next time round is a list-hygiene issue, not a copy issue — run the target list through Snov verification before send.

The combined "ideal email" recipe

Best-of-both-worlds template pulled from both winners:

Subject

  • Verb-led, under 45 characters, contains one hard number, no emoji or ALL-CAPS. (Email A pattern)

Opening

  • Start with the reader's problem (A) — but if the list is broad, prefix with quantified authority (B, adapted): "From working with 40+ UK universities, we know…" then the pain.
  • Use a clean merge with fallback: "Hi {{first_name|there}},". Never expose raw merge syntax.

Body

  • 150–200 words maximum. Under a 45-second read.
  • Bulleted pain list is fine (B) — cap at 3 bullets, not 5. Long lists read as complaint sheets.
  • Layer vocabulary by intended persona mix: Research primary, Finance/IT secondary if the list is mixed (B strategy). Single-persona if the list is one institution (A strategy).
  • Name the product — actual name, not "modern Cloud SaaS platforms" (A).
  • Attach a hard number to the promise. Repeat it once as a standalone highlighted line (A).

CTA

  • One text link. Low-friction ask — "share a short overview", not "book a 30-minute demo".
  • Never a homepage URL (fix B). Always a conversation link.

Send

  • Verify the list before send — target under 2% bounce. (B breached; A did not)
  • Tight lists outperform broadcasts on rate. Broadcasts win on volume. Run both — score them differently.

Honest limits

  • Email B's subject line was not supplied for review — a poor subject can drag a strong body down, and vice versa.
  • Reply / enquiry data is not in this dataset for either email. Everything above is a proxy — clicks, not booked calls.
  • Sample of two winners is enough to spot a pattern, not to claim statistical proof. Keep tearing down each new high-performer to widen the evidence base.
