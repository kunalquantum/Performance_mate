How this email was picked

From 12 tracked outbound campaign stages in campaign_batches.csv. Filters applied to isolate a repeatable pattern (not a lucky one-off):

  • Excluded event / conference promotions — ARMA, NCURA, "come by our booth" sends behave differently from product outreach.
  • Excluded celebration / anniversary / seasonal copy — these are exceptions to the working pattern.
  • Minimum 10 recipients — so the open rate reflects the writing, not a 3-person test blast.
  • Ranked by open rate. Winner: Ulster Base at 56.5% (23 recipients, 13 opens, 13 clicks).
Runner-up: GFZ Helmholtz Base at 60% but only 10 sends — Ulster wins on statistical weight and it is the only email in the set where 100% of openers clicked.

What the subject does right

  • Verb-led. Starts with "Improve" — an action the reader wants, not a claim about us.
  • Benefit, not feature. "Funding opportunity matching" is the outcome; the product isn't named.
  • Hard number. "25%" grounds the promise — a percentage always out-scrolls adjectives ("dramatically", "significantly").
  • No noise. No emoji, no ALL-CAPS, no brackets, no "Re:", no first-name-in-subject trick.
  • Under 45 characters — fits inside the mobile inbox preview without truncation.

Body — structure map

Six paragraphs, each doing one job. The order — problem → trend → social proof → product → number reprise → soft CTA — is the working template.

1 · Greeting
"Good afternoon, [First name],"
Personal token in the salutation, no "Hi/Hey/Hello" — matches the sender's formal-consultative register.
2 · The pain
"The increasing volume of competitive funding opportunities makes manual tracking increasingly challenging, with relevant opportunities often missed or identified too late."
Opens with the reader's problem, not the product. Names two failure states — "missed" and "too late" — the reader nods along.
3 · The shift
"AI-driven automation is beginning to change this by helping universities identify and align targeted funding opportunities with strategic research themes and institutional priorities..."
Positions a category, not a product. Reader is first educated about the market shift.
4 · Social proof
"In conversations with several universities, we have heard that existing systems do not always provide the level of intelligent and effective opportunity matching they require."
Soft proof — "we have heard" — no logo drop, no case-study name, just implied consensus. Lowers the guard.
5 · Product + number
"GrantsNow's AI Driven Funder Opportunity Scanner was designed specifically to address this challenge, with institutions reporting improvements in funding opportunity matching of over 25%."
Product named for the first time — paragraph 5, not paragraph 1. Attached immediately to the number.
6 · Number reprise
Improvement in funding opportunity matching: over 25%.
Same number, standalone line. Bold visual anchor. Skimmers see this even if they only glance.
7 · Soft CTA
"We would be happy to share a high-level overview if you feel our platform would provide you with additional funding opportunities. Connect with us"
Low-friction — "share a high-level overview", not "book a 30-minute demo". CTA reads as a text link, not a button demand.
8 · Sign-off
"Regards,
Zoe
GrantsNow"
Single first name + brand. Person-to-person, not "The GrantsNow Team".

Why this matters

The copy layers Research primary + IT-Systems secondary vocabulary. Ulster's contact base is dominantly Research, so the dominant language pulls the biggest audience. The IT vocabulary ("AI-driven", "automation", "platform") is enough to signal technical credibility without alienating a Research reader — a useful hedge on this kind of institution.

Zero Finance vocabulary — no "cost", "audit", "budget", "compliance". If Finance is the target next time, the copy needs re-writing.

The recipe — rules distilled for the next email

Subject line

  • Under 45 characters so mobile preview does not truncate.
  • Open with a verb the reader wants (Improve, Reduce, Speed up, Free…).
  • Include one hard number — %, ×, hours, days. No adjectives like "significantly".
  • No emoji, no ALL-CAPS, no exclamation.
  • Do not name the product — sell the outcome.

Body structure

  • Six body paragraphs, each with one job: Pain → Trend → Social proof → Product → Number reprise → Soft CTA.
  • Open with the reader's problem — not the product, not the company.
  • Reserve the product name until paragraph 4 or 5.
  • Repeat the headline number as a standalone highlighted line so skimmers still catch it.
  • Use "we have heard" style social proof if you cannot name a customer.
  • ~150–200 words. Under a 45-second read.

Call to action

  • One CTA only — a single hyperlink at the bottom.
  • Low-friction ask — "share a high-level overview", not "book a 30-min demo".
  • Text link, not a big coloured button.

Voice

  • Consultative and evidence-led. Zero exclamation marks. Zero "amazing / dramatic / revolutionary" adjectives.
  • Restrained "you" — use it inside the CTA line only.
  • Signed by one named person + brand. Not "The GrantsNow Team".
  • Match vocabulary to the dominant recipient persona. Research audience → Research language. Finance audience → Finance language. IT audience → IT language.

Send parameters

  • Tight list — one institution or one persona-slice at a time. Small sends outperform big blasts.
  • Personalize the salutation with a first-name token. Only one token — don't sprinkle brackets through the body.
  • Send Thursday afternoon is what worked here; Tuesday / Wednesday afternoons are the usual alternatives.
  • Only email to a clean list — the winner delivered 100%. Bounces above 3% hurt everything.

Honest limits of this analysis

  • The sample is small — 10 non-event candidates. This is the best pattern we can see in the data today, not a statistically validated formula.
  • Ulster's contact base was already Research-dominant and email-verified — the list quality is part of the result, not just the copy.
  • Reply data was not captured for this send (ESP export blank). Enquiries — the real objective — cannot be confirmed from this dataset.
  • The Base stage of a sequence typically outperforms Follow-ups. This is a first-touch template. Follow-up templates need their own tear-down.
