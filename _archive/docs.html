02See the year at a glance

A single scatter chart puts every post in one view. Score on the vertical, date on the horizontal. Higher and to the right is more recent and better.

Scatter view. Click a dot to inspect it.
  • A.
    Full-year scatter with clickable dots
    Each post is one dot. Teal dots beat a normal post. Muted dots did not. Hover shows first line, date, score. Click to compare it against the top performer.
    Spot outliers, streaks, and slumps in three seconds without opening a spreadsheet.
  • B.
    Median line across the year
    A dotted horizontal line marks the median score for the current filter. Everything above it is worth studying, everything below is worth questioning.
    Give the reader a fair baseline without doing the maths in their head.
  • C.
    Five KPI tiles above the chart
    Posts in view, total views, views vs a normal post, reactions vs a normal post, top score. Recomputes as filters change.
    A pocket dashboard for the exec who has 20 seconds.

03Compare a post to the top performer

Two cards, side by side. The one you picked on the left. The top-scoring post on the right. Every attribute lined up so differences are obvious.

Compare view. Teal values on the right are where the top post differs from your pick.
  • A.
    Side-by-side post cards
    Each card shows hook, date, views, likes, comments, and the eight attributes that matter most. Same layout on both sides so nothing is hidden.
    The reader can see the difference between winner and average without translating anything.
  • B.
    Same-topic-only toggle
    Restricts the right card to posts on the same topic as the left. A whitepaper stops being compared to an event post.
    Comparisons stay fair. Beating a normal whitepaper is a real signal, beating an ARMA week is not.
  • C.
    Prev and Next under each card
    Walk through the ranking one post at a time. Left card walks all posts, right card walks the topic-locked list.
    Quick way to sanity-check whether the top score is a one-off or the start of a pattern.

04Read the ideal-post recipe

Averaged from the top quartile of posts in the current filter. Not a prediction, a description of what has actually worked.

The recipe. Copy the whole block as text and paste it into a brief.
  • A.
    A one-glance recipe card
    Topic, ask, length, publish day, format, media type, image subject, palette, face in image, target region, word count, first-line length, hashtag count, hashtag set.
    One card the creative team can act on. No pivot tables, no data terminology.
  • B.
    Copy as text and download as .txt
    Every recipe is copyable in a plain-text version formatted as a proper brief. Paste directly into a copywriter ticket or a designer brief.
    Removes the manual step of retyping. The recipe travels with the ticket.
  • C.
    Warning when the recipe is not repeatable
    If more than 40 percent of the top-scoring posts are event posts, a yellow warning appears telling you the recipe cannot be built on a normal week.
    Prevents chasing a signal you cannot execute on. ARMA works because it is ARMA.

05Score the photo before you publish

Upload the image. The tool detects palette, face presence, and whether the image has text overlay. It scores the visual side on its own contribution to the predicted post score.

Photo scorecard. Auto-detected details on top, the biggest single change to lift the score at the bottom.
  • A.
    Auto-detected palette, face, and text overlay
    Uploads run through K-means clustering for palette, an OpenCV face detector, and an edge-density check for text overlays. All three fill the dropdowns for you.
    The designer does not have to manually classify the image. Fifteen seconds instead of two minutes per draft.
  • B.
    Photo contribution as a signed score
    Shows the exact points this photo adds to (or subtracts from) the baseline. Not a mystery number. Photo contribution plus text contribution equals the combined score, exactly.
    The designer sees whether their image is helping or hurting before the post ships.
  • C.
    The single change that would lift it most
    The top visual swap that would raise the score, ranked. For example: image or text becomes video or document, +14.3 points.
    Turns the score from a verdict into an action.

06Score the caption at the same time

Paste the caption. Word count, hashtag count, question and link presence, length band are all derived automatically. Topic, ask, and publish day are the only manual picks.

Text scorecard. Every metric on the right is derived from the caption on the left.
  • A.
    Auto-derived text metrics
    Word count, first-line length, hashtag count, hashtags themselves, question presence, link presence, and length band come from the pasted caption.
    The copywriter never has to count. The tool watches the caption and reacts.
  • B.
    Text contribution as a signed score
    The exact points the caption adds to (or subtracts from) the baseline. Contribution and photo add up to the combined prediction, no gap.
    The copywriter sees whether the caption is doing the work, or the photo is carrying it.
  • C.
    Top single change to lift the caption
    Ranked improvements limited to text-side features. Moves like publish-day, ask, or topic switches.
    A specific action instead of vague advice like "try harder".

07Predict the whole draft together

Combines photo and text into one prediction. Three tiles for score, views, reaction rate. The three past posts closest to your draft for a sanity-check.

Combined prediction. The equation on top shows the exact split between photo and text.
  • A.
    Additive scorecard equation
    Baseline plus photo plus text equals the combined prediction, exactly. Uses a Shapley decomposition, honest at the maths level.
    The reader can trace exactly where the number came from. No black box.
  • B.
    Three prediction tiles
    Predicted score, predicted views, predicted reaction rate. Trained on the current filter.
    Not just a rank. A concrete expectation on the three numbers stakeholders ask about.
  • C.
    Three most similar past posts
    The nearest past posts by attribute overlap. Shows each with its real score, views, and interactions.
    Sanity-check the prediction against posts you can actually go read.
  • D.
    Top overall single-change suggestions
    Across photo and text combined, ranked by predicted lift.
    One list of the biggest levers. No prioritising by hand.

08Get playbook-mixed hashtags

Upload the GrantsNow hashtag workbook. The Recommend page then follows the playbook rule: one Broad + two Mid-tier + one Targeted + #GrantsNow. Every suggestion carries its role and past performance.

Playbook recipe. Each pick shows role, tier, source category, and past performance.
  • A.
    Full library import
    The GrantsNow workbook loads all 148 hashtags across 10 categories in one drop. Category structure is auto-detected.
    The playbook the team already agreed on now drives the recommender. No re-typing.
  • B.
    Mix rule enforced
    Exactly one Broad + two Mid-tier + one Targeted + #GrantsNow. Total: 4 to 5 tags per post, matching the workbook's own guidance.
    The team's own playbook is honoured every time. No ad-hoc lists that drift from the strategy.
  • C.
    Ranked by past performance
    Within each segment, hashtags used in higher-scoring past posts float up. Unproven tags default to the topic's own median, not an arbitrary number, and are tiebroken by the workbook's tier.
    Fresh hashtags still surface. Proven ones do not lose their spot to made-up numbers.
  • D.
    Editable topic to category mapping
    Every topic has a multiselect of the loaded categories. Change what pain_point pulls from, and the recipe re-ranks live.
    The default mapping is opinionated. Now the team can argue with it without opening a file.
  • E.
    Append to caption in one click
    The whole recipe drops into the caption box. Text metrics and hashtag count refresh automatically.
    The recipe becomes part of the draft in a click, not a copy-paste dance.

09Nine charts on nine tabs

The Visualize screen. Every chart is filter-aware. Use for weekly review, stakeholder presentations, or deep-dives.

The tab strip. Nine categories of chart in one screen.
  • 01
    Overview
    KPIs, score distribution, and weekly cadence with median-score line overlay.
    Where the year sits at a single glance.
  • 02
    Time
    Calendar heatmap, rolling 4-week average, monthly volume, score distribution by publish day.
    Reveals seasonality and identifies dead weeks.
  • 03
    Content strategy
    Day-by-format matrix with in-cell values, six median-score bar charts (topic, ask, publish day, length, palette, face), topic-by-outcome heatmap.
    Says which content shape is doing the winning.
  • 04
    Reach & engagement
    Reach against engagement scatter with topic colour and click size. 2D post density. Pareto curve of cumulative views.
    Distinguishes distribution problems from writing problems.
  • 05
    Caption signals
    Word count vs score with smoothed trend, hashtag count vs reactions, box plots for question and link presence, emoji count.
    Answers "does length hurt?" and "do emojis help?" with real data, not opinion.
  • 06
    What wins
    Attribute lift with confidence opacity, distinctive words, distinctive hashtags, top-versus-bottom side-by-side table.
    One glance shows the specific attribute values that beat a normal post.
  • 07
    Matched pairs (A/B)
    Posts sharing the same first line clustered as pairs. Shows what varied and what stayed the same.
    Real A/B pairs are the cleanest cause-and-effect evidence in the dataset.
  • 08
    Model insights
    Feature importance chart from the trained predictor plus per-post contribution table for any post you pick.
    Makes the recommender's reasoning legible instead of opaque.
  • 09
    Correlations
    Full correlation matrix of every numeric feature, plus parallel coordinates showing winners as teal polylines.
    Spot the pairs of variables that move together, and the shape a winning post traces.

10Find real A/B evidence in the archive

Some posts share the exact same first line. When that happens, the tool clusters them and shows the score gap. This is the cleanest causal evidence the archive contains.

  • A.
    Automatic cluster detection
    Any post with a shared 80-character prefix in the first line gets clustered. In the current archive, 21 clusters cover 47 posts.
    Repost and A/B behaviour surfaces without human review.
  • B.
    Cluster-level score comparison
    Every cluster gets a dot chart with the two or three variants ranked side by side, plus a table showing dates, scores, views, likes and comments.
    The team sees which visual variant, timing, or format actually did better within a fair comparison.

11See what drives the score

The Model insights tab opens up the recommender. Feature importance globally, plus a per-post breakdown for any specific post.

  • A.
    Feature importance chart
    Ranks every model input by weight. Currently word count and day of week lead the ranking, meaning length and timing carry most of the signal.
    The team can prioritise the levers that actually move the score.
  • B.
    Per-post feature contribution
    Pick any post from a dropdown. See how each of its features individually pushed the predicted score up or down.
    Every prediction gets an explanation. No number appears without a why.

12Filters that cascade through every screen

Every filter is a "remove" filter, not a "keep" filter. Trim specific distortions rather than opting in from scratch.

Filter groups. Each group collapses under an expander in the sidebar.
  • A.
    Skip event posts checkbox
    One click removes anything tagged as event_presence, since event weeks distort the ranking.
    See what the page does on a normal week, not on ARMA.
  • B.
    Content filters
    Remove topics, remove asks, remove publish days, remove length bands. Each is a multiselect.
    Focus the analysis on the content mix that matches this week's decision.
  • C.
    Visual filters
    Remove specific palettes, remove face y/n, remove text-on-image y/n, remove media types. Only appears when the tagging is loaded.
    Isolate visual patterns. Answer "how do posts without a face perform?" in one click.
  • D.
    Campaign filter
    Drop entire campaigns (whitepaper - proposal reviews, demo - AI integration, and so on).
    Compare campaign performance without the noise of the others.
  • E.
    Clear all filters button
    Appears whenever any filter is active. Resets everything to the full-year view in one click.
    No hunt-and-peck to undo a set of filters that turned out unhelpful.

13Date range with four presets

The most-used filter lives on the main view, not tucked in the sidebar.

  • A.
    Two date pickers
    Custom start and end. If you invert them, the tool swaps.
    The reader picks the exact window that matches the decision on the table.
  • B.
    Four preset buttons
    30 days. 90 days. 6 months. Full year. One click each.
    Weekly reviews, quarterly reviews, and annual reviews all reachable in a single click.

14Signal weights the team can argue with

The score is a judgement. Four sliders make it editable so nobody has to accept a black box.

  • A.
    Comments, reposts, clicks, likes sliders
    Default weights: 6 for comments, 4 for reposts, 2 for clicks, 1 for likes. Move any slider, the whole ranking updates.
    If the team disagrees with the default, they can adjust it in seconds, not through a code change.

15Known limits

Honest gotchas
  • LinkedIn export has no time of day. Best-time-to-post is impossible to answer. The tool does not draw a chart it cannot honestly fill.
  • Dwell time is not in the export. A post read slowly and a post scrolled past look the same. Comment weight is higher than like weight partly to compensate.
  • The recommender trains on 125 posts. Predictions are directional, not oracular. The three most similar past posts sit next to every prediction so you can sanity-check.
  • Face detection needs OpenCV 4.x. When unavailable, palette and text-on-image still work. A red note tells you when face detection is off.
  • Layer 3 tagging covers 66 of 125 posts. Visual filters apply cleanly to that 66. Growing the coverage is the highest-value hour of manual work available.
